# Team Leader Job Analysis

**Defining Frontline Leadership Through Evidence**

## Project Overview

Team Leader was one of the most common frontline leadership roles across Rock City Enterprises, with positions spanning Food, Retail, and Support Services. Because Team Leaders operated across very different business units, expectations for the role were not always consistent.

Working in partnership with HR, the Team Leader position was selected for a comprehensive job analysis because of both its prevalence across the organization and its importance to day-to-day operations.

The project examined what Team Leaders actually needed to do to perform successfully, which knowledge, skills, abilities, and other characteristics (KSAOs) were critical to performance, and which capabilities employees needed upon entering the role. The findings were intended to create greater clarity and consistency while providing an evidence-based foundation for decisions related to selection, training, performance management, and role design.

## Business Context

At the time of the project, Rock City Enterprises was evaluating the structure of its frontline leadership roles. The organization operated across multiple business segments with differing operational environments, staffing demands, and leadership structures.

One particular area under consideration was the Senior Team Leader position. This role had developed over time in response to operational needs, but the organization was beginning to reconsider how it fit within the broader leadership structure.

Rather than assuming that Team Leader responsibilities were consistent across the organization, the job analysis provided an opportunity to define the work using evidence from employees and leaders who understood the role firsthand.

## Approach

The project used a multi-method job analysis process combining organizational context, qualitative discovery, and quantitative data.

The process included:

- Review of existing job descriptions and relevant O*NET occupational information
- Stakeholder consultation with organizational leadership
- Subject Matter Expert (SME) interviews across Food, Retail, and Support Services
- Development of a Job Analysis Questionnaire (JAQ)
- Collection of task frequency and importance ratings
- Evaluation of KSAO importance and necessity at hire
- Quantitative identification of critical tasks and KSAOs
- Task-to-KSAO linkage analysis
- Review of critical incidents and qualitative findings
- Translation of findings into recommendations for organizational use

The JAQ included **12 task statements and 22 KSAOs** developed from the qualitative discovery process. Thirty-two SMEs representing Team Leaders, supervisors, and frontline employees participated in questionnaire administration.

Using both qualitative and quantitative information allowed the team to examine not only what employees described as important, but whether those themes were supported across a broader group of SMEs. The two methods showed substantial overlap, particularly around leadership, role modeling, shift management, adaptability, communication, and interpersonal effectiveness.

## Key Findings

The analysis identified **eight critical tasks** associated with successful Team Leader performance, including:

- Demonstrating workplace behaviors aligned with organizational values
- Maintaining safety and quality standards
- Supervising daily work during a shift
- Providing a positive guest experience
- Ensuring compliance with organizational policies and procedures
- Collaborating with managers, vendors, and peers
- Monitoring inventory
- Conducting on-the-job training

The analysis also identified **12 critical KSAOs**, including dependability, customer service, oral communication, work ethic, active listening, problem-solving and critical thinking, self-control, leadership and team building, adaptability, empathy, organization, and time management.

Although operational requirements differed somewhat across Food, Retail, and Support Services, the findings demonstrated a common foundation for successful frontline leadership across the organization.

## Organizational Application

One of the most valuable outcomes of the project emerged as Rock City Enterprises continued evaluating its frontline leadership structure.

The job analysis demonstrated that employees serving in Senior Team Leader positions could not necessarily be treated as equivalent to standard Team Leaders. Senior Team Leaders were performing additional administrative responsibilities that were not identified as critical components of the standard Team Leader role.

As the organization subsequently restructured these positions, the findings helped inform the distinction between **Team Leader 1 and Team Leader 2**, allowing additional responsibilities to remain differentiated rather than simply combining employees performing meaningfully different work into a single role.

More broadly, the analysis provided an evidence-based foundation that could support:

- Selection and hiring criteria
- Training and development priorities
- Performance expectations
- Job descriptions
- Role differentiation
- Leadership development

The project demonstrated how job analysis can move beyond documenting responsibilities and become a tool for making more defensible talent and organizational decisions.

## Reflection

One of the accomplishments I am most proud of is the scope of what our team completed in less than three months. The project required coordinating organizational leaders, SMEs across multiple business segments, interviews, questionnaire administration, quantitative analysis, linkage analysis, written deliverables, and a final stakeholder presentation.

More importantly, the work had implications for real employees and helped provide greater clarity around differences in frontline leadership responsibilities.

With additional time and organizational access, I would expand the initial discovery phase. Although we understood that changes to the Senior Team Leader structure were being considered, our project team did not have complete visibility into the organization's evolving workforce strategy. Earlier conversations with HR and senior leaders about the anticipated future state could have helped us frame the analysis and recommendations even more strategically.

I would also build a stronger communication plan for closing the loop with participating SMEs. Employees contributed meaningful time and expertise to the analysis, and where organizational confidentiality allows, participants should have an opportunity to understand how their input informed decisions about their work.
